# Threat model

## Scope and assumptions

Use fictional information and authorized references. Preserve copyright on standards and label policy, risk, audit and continuity examples as educational.

Use the proposed [component graph](architecture.md) as the system boundary. Assumptions and control efficacy are subject to laboratory verification.

| Threat | Trust boundary | Proposed control | Future verification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier outage | Provider to order portal | Recovery objectives, backups and dependency inventory | Tabletop tests alternate intake and restore evidence |
| Account takeover | External users to SaaS identity | MFA, access reviews and incident escalation | Review fictional coverage denominator and escalation ownership |
| Ransomware impact | Endpoints to shared records | Segmentation and tested restore | Tabletop links assumptions and owner to decision |
| Bias or tampering | Assessor to approver | Versioned register, independent review and restricted approval | Changes have rationale, timestamp and signer |

## Residual risk and review triggers

Use ISO for management and controls, CSF for outcome communication and CIS for actionable safeguards. Example mappings convey intent and do not imply equivalence. Use EBIOS workshops for scenarios with meaningful threat and ecosystem dependencies. Keep inherent and residual scores separate since a planned control cannot reduce observed residual risk.

Revisit the model whenever the topology, identities, dataset, or deployment context changes.
